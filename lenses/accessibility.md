# Lens: accessibility

**Question.** Can every person operate what changed: by keyboard, with a screen reader, at any zoom? Its author rarely meets the failure, so it is read against the W3C's checks and WCAG.

**Read first.** The project's interface paths the change touches, then the components they render.

**Checklist.**

1. Does each page carry its own title and one heading order?
2. Does each image and icon control carry a text alternative?
3. Do text and each control's edge meet WCAG's contrast?
4. Can each control be reached and used by keyboard, in reading order, with focus visible?
5. After an action, a dialog or a page change, where does focus land?
6. Does each field have a label, and each error name its field and the fix?
7. Does the page reflow at 320 CSS pixels wide without losing content?
8. Does motion that is not essential stop under the reduced-motion setting?
9. Is a status announced to a screen reader without moving focus?

**Not its business.** The look, the copy's taste.

**Silent.** Yes: the page works for its author and fails for the person who needs it.

**Applies.** declared.

**Run by.** auditor.
